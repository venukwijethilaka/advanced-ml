# Week 01 – Introduction to ML 

## Key concepts- 

Concepts are started with understanding what intelligence means. It means being able to learn, reason, solve problems, and adapt. We looked at the different subfields of Artificial Intelligence such as Natural Language Processing (NLP), Symbolic AI, Computer Vision.

Traditional programming works by taking data and running a program on them to get the desired result. However, in Machine Learning (ML) we do just the opposite! we feed data and our desired results into the algorithm and let it figure out what kind of program could do that.

Another key insight was that there are two kinds of relationships between variables in data called deterministic and probabilistic. Deterministic simply means “given some input, my output will be this”, while in probabilistic things aren’t certain but more likely to happen, likewise you have some randomness causing your data points to scatter around a nice little curve.

the functional relationship like in a simple temperature alarm, writing down the rules isn’t too bad. But if we don’t know the underlying functions or they’re really complicated like in image recognition, you can turn to ML algorithms to help us come up with estimations based on our labelled training data. At the core of everything in ML is a decision process (how do we make these predictions?), some way of measuring errors, then finally the process used to optimize those parameters.

We touched upon several different subdomains within ML such as Supervised, Unsupervised, Semi-supervised, Reinforcement learning.

Finally we discussed Artificial Neural Networks and their inspiration from biology. They consist of layers of interconnected units each performing some operation on incoming values through non-linear activation functions. These networks are trained using some optimization procedure in order to make good decisions when given inputs.
